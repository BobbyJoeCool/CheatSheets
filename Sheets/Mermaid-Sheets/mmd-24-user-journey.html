Sheet 24

User Journey

journey · title · section · task : score : actors · 1–5 scores

How a Task Feels

A journey diagram maps the steps a user takes to finish a goal and how each step feels. Steps are grouped into sections; each task line is Task name: score: actors, where the score runs from 1 (bad) to 5 (great) and the actors are a comma-separated list. Mermaid draws faces and colors from the scores, so low points stand out at a glance.

Notes

  • Score must be a whole number from 1 to 5
  • Several actors per task: Pay: 2: Me, Bank
  • Each actor gets a color and a legend entry
  • Sections run left to right in order

Buying a Ticket

journey
  title Buy a concert ticket
  section Find
    Search events: 4: Fan
    Compare seats: 3: Fan
  section Pay
    Enter card: 2: Fan
    3-D Secure check: 1: Fan, Bank
  section After
    Get e-ticket: 5: Fan
    Add to wallet: 4: Fan

⚠ Common Mistakes

  • Using scores outside 1–5 or decimals (3.5). The parser rejects them.
  • Forgetting the second colon: Search: Fan has no score, so the line fails.
  • Packing in every click. A journey is about the shape of the experience; ten steps per section is too many.

✓ Tips & Best Practices

  • Base scores on research (interviews, support tickets), not guesses, and note the source in the doc.
  • Add the system as an actor on steps where it's involved (Fan, Bank) to show who causes the pain.
  • Pair a journey with a flowchart: the journey shows feelings, the flowchart shows the logic.

Quick Summary

  • journey + title — declaration
  • section — phases of the journey
  • Task: score: actors — one step
  • 1–5 — score drives faces and colors
