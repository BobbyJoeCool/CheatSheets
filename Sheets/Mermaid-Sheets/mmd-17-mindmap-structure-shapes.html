Indentation Is the Structure

A mindmap grows branches out of one root idea. There are no arrows to write: indentation alone decides which node is a child of which. Each line is one node; the text can sit in brackets that choose its shape, the same idea as flowchart shapes but with a slightly different set. Mindmaps are laid out with the cose-bilkent force layout by default, and each top-level branch gets its own color.

Rules

  • Exactly one root: the first node, least indented
  • Deeper indentation = child of the line above it
  • Indent consistently — spaces, not a mix of tabs and spaces
  • Optional id before the shape: n1[Text]
  • Not in the tiny build

⚠ Common Mistakes

  • Adding a second root by out-denting a later line. Mermaid throws an error; there can be only one root.
  • Mixing tabs and spaces. Two lines that look aligned can parse at different depths.
  • Writing --> arrows between nodes. Mindmaps have no link syntax; hierarchy comes only from indentation.

✓ Tips & Best Practices

  • Indent with two spaces per level and turn on visible whitespace in your editor.
  • Use shapes for meaning, not decoration: e.g. hexagons for risks, clouds for open questions.
  • Keep labels to a few words; long sentences make the force layout spread out.
