What Kind of Problem Is It?

A cynefin-beta diagram draws Dave Snowden's Cynefin sense-making framework: five domains that call for different responses. Clear (best practice), Complicated (analyze, good practice), Complex (probe, emergent practice), Chaotic (act first) and Confusion in the middle. Open a domain with its keyword and list quoted items under it; --> lines show items moving between domains.

Notes

  • Items are quoted strings, one per line
  • Confusion shows up to 3 items, then +N more
  • The "cliff" sits between Clear and Chaotic
  • Empty domains still render — handy as a worksheet
  • Added in v11.16

Common Transitions

Move Means
complex --> complicated Pattern understood
complicated --> clear Practice codified
chaotic --> complex Crisis stabilized
clear --> chaotic Complacency (cliff)

⚠ Common Mistakes

  • Leaving items unquoted. Each item must be a quoted string on its own line.
  • Putting long lists in one domain. Quadrants have a fixed size, and long lists overflow.
  • Treating Confusion as a fifth quadrant; it's the state of not knowing, and items should move out of it.

✓ Tips & Best Practices

  • Use an empty Cynefin diagram as a workshop template, then fill it in together.
  • Write items as situations ("Unknown failure"), not actions, so the domain decides the response.
  • Add transitions to show how a problem's domain changed over an incident.
