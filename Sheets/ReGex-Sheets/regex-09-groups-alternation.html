Grouping and Choosing

Parentheses do two jobs: they group part of a pattern so a quantifier or alternation applies to all of it, and they capture the text that part matched so you can read it back or reuse it. | means "or" and has the lowest precedence of any operator, so it splits the entire pattern (or the enclosing group) into alternatives. Use a non-capturing group (?:…) when you need grouping but not the captured text.

Rules

  • Groups are numbered by their opening parenthesis, left to right, starting at 1
  • Group 0 is the whole match
  • A group that didn't take part in the match returns None / null / empty
  • [abc] beats a|b|c for single characters: faster and clearer

Groups & Alternation (Python)

import re
m = re.search(r"(\d{4})-(\d{2})-(\d{2})", "Due 2026-10-10")
m.group(0)                                  # '2026-10-10'
m.group(1), m.group(3)                      # ('2026', '10')
m.groups()                                  # ('2026', '10', '10')
re.findall(r"^cat|dog$", "cat or dog")      # ['cat', 'dog']
re.findall(r"^(?:cat|dog)$", "cat or dog")  # []
re.findall(r"(cat|dog)s?", "cats dog")      # ['cat', 'dog']
re.findall(r"(?:cat|dog)s?", "cats dog")    # ['cats', 'dog']
re.search(r"((a)(b))", "ab").groups()       # ('ab', 'a', 'b')
re.search(r"(x)?y", "y").group(1)           # None

Code Explained

(\d{4}) — captures the year as group 1
^cat|dog$ is (^cat)|(dog$), not "cat or dog as the whole string"
^(?:cat|dog)$ — the group keeps the anchors around both choices
findall returns group text when the pattern has groups; (?:…) avoids that
Nested ((a)(b)) — outer group is 1, then 2 and 3
(x)? didn't participate, so group 1 is None

⚠ Common Mistakes

  • Unwrapped alternation with anchors: ^yes|no$ accepts “yes please” and “say no”; wrap it: ^(?:yes|no)$
  • Adding a group for grouping only and shifting every later group number; use (?:…)
  • Expecting findall to return full matches when the pattern has a capture group

✓ Tips & Best Practices

  • Default to (?:…) and capture only what you'll read back
  • Use named groups once a pattern has more than two or three captures
  • Factor shared prefixes: \b(?:Jan|Feb|Mar)\b rather than repeating \b in each branch

Quick Summary

  • (…) — group + capture
  • (?:…) — group only
  • | — lowest precedence: splits the whole group
  • group(0) — the entire match
