Organizations and Teams

An organization owns repos for a group, with owners (full control), members and billing managers. Teams group members, get access to repos, and can be @mentioned (@org/team) to notify everyone or request reviews. Teams can be nested: child teams inherit the parent's repo access. The audit log records every org-level change.

Org Facts

  • Keep at least two owners so the org can't be orphaned
  • Team visibility: Visible (all members) or Secret
  • Free orgs: unlimited public/private repos; some features need Team

Who Can Do This

  • Role: Org owner · team maintainers manage their team
  • Plan: Free (team sync & audit API: Enterprise)
  • Repos: public and private

Set Up an Org

  1. + → New organization, pick the Free plan
  2. People → Invite member; require 2FA under Authentication security
  3. Teams → New team, e.g. backend, reviewers
  4. Give teams repo access (team → Repositories → Add)
  5. Member privileges: base permission Read, limit repo creation
  6. Use @org/team in CODEOWNERS and review requests

Automate It

Add a member to a team (REST)
curl -X PUT -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" \
  https://api.github.com/orgs/ORG/teams/backend/memberships/USER \
  -d '{"role":"member"}'

⚠ Common Mistakes

  • One owner only: if they leave, nobody can manage billing or settings
  • Base permission Write: every member can push to every repo
  • Inviting people before requiring 2FA: enforce it first

✓ Tips & Best Practices

  • Mirror your real structure with nested teams (e.g. eng → eng-frontend)
  • Use a reviewers team in CODEOWNERS instead of named people
  • Filter the audit log by action:repo.destroy to find deletions

Quick Summary
